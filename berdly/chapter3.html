Chapter 3

Late Night (Reading)

(Hmm... it's getting late. I haven't gotten much done...)

After an hour or so of inconsistent homework completion, I start to close up my notebooks and put them away. I fight my worries about remaining work as I slide my materials back into my backpack. I lift my arms from the zipped-up opening and plop them on the table. I resist the urge to lay my head down as I look around to assess my situation.

I fiddle with various objects while deciding whether my day is completely done. The sides of the desk are adorned with books I haven't read enough of and objects on top of them that I don't want to move. My lamp is a little too yellow, but it's fine enough not to bother me. My calculator is on the corner of the surface, lined up perfectly with its edges. The clock's ticking suddenly becomes the loudest thing in the room.

 

Before I know it, I'm fingering the pages of a book without really thinking. My eyes absorb the words as is their unstoppable wont:

"Chapter 3: Properties of Negative Readings"

"This state of light is most physically peculiar in that it does not act simply like the absence of, or as a simple counterpart of, light itself."

(W-huh? Light? What book is this, Opticks?)

I shut the book and turn it in confusion. (Ah, it's that... weird one.) I turn to my only landmark in the book: Chapter 5. I read the first few paragraphs, in full this time.

Sentence by sentence, I grow more surprised by how familiar the "Effects of Darkness" sound to me. (Fantastical events... like a game... remembered as dreams... this is exactly like...)

 

"I-I must be going crazy," I blurt out loud. Startled by my outburst, I look around my room hastily. After my alertness levels returned to normal, I scrambled for more confirmation. (This book can't just be made to... uh, trick me, right? To be crazy nonsense?) I looked through the table of contents for anything about what causes this sort of thing, what I assume is the outcome of the "Negative Readings".

(Intro, no. Readings, effects, physics, tests, no, no, no...)

Suddenly, there it is. "Sources". (Yes!) I study the chapter, but I begin to understand almost none of the things being said. Seems like a lot of terms have been made up in the book for convenience. After a few minutes, I begin the real reading as I take to the index.

 

The minute hand does a 180 as I peruse the knowledge provided by the great _.____t_r. Understanding just a little more brings me some weird sort of catharsis. By the time I close the book, I feel like I'm going to faint. I lean on my bed, trying to think more clearly.

(What's... what's the sharpest thing I've got?)

 

. . .

 

I open my eyes and see the carpet, or what looks like the carpet. Its fibers look more lush than ever, like every one is a large grass in a field or a tree in a forest. I can hardly see them in the dark blurry air. A smudge of darkness begins to show itself in the array of off-white lumps. As I focus my eyes, it gets clearer, like an inkblot on—

(Wait—) I squint. I pivot my arm around myself and present to myself my hand. In it is what once was a perfectly good pen. (Crap.)

I get up and instinctively try to rub the ink off of my hand, to my own chagrin as it simply dirties my other hand along with.

(Believing some stupid prank book...) I think while washing my hands in the bathroom. (Not like me. I'm so stupid. Why would I— But it was so real, I thought—) I quit the self-quibbling and head back to my room. After a moment, I grab the doorhandle and turn it. My hand hesitates to turn it. I walk in, looking down at the carpet to face my redolent shame—

 

(What the—?!) To my shock, the ink staining the carpet is lifting itself into the air as if its density is slightly less than that of air. I snap my eyes away and back several times in pursuit of confirmation that this ridiculous event is actually happening in my room. Despite my doubtful glances, the spot keeps jaunting languidly upwards. As if I was trying to show up the particle in speed, I dashed to the book on the table.

I flipped the pages, somewhat sloppily, to somewhere in Chapter 3 or Chapter 4, a section detailling the appearance of that substance which Chapter 3 is about. The author, as if hearing my questions, writes:

"If one sees smoke or dark spots in the air, do not worry. There is not a fire nearby, and you have not suddenly developed cataracts;* what you are seeing is the Darkness. The negative readings show themselves to us patches or bodies of darkness, amorphous and flowing upwards as if lighter than air." (Hey, I said that!) "A substance like this is a sign darkness is being produced.

 

* The author cannot, as of the printing of this edition, confirm whether the reader has or has not rapidly developed cataracts, or if a fire has or has not broken out near the reader in his or her time reading this passage."
